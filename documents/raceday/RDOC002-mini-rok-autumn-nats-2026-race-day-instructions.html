01 Welcome

Introduction

Welcome to the Mini ROK class. We are pleased to have you with us for a weekend of proper, competitive racing and another important step in the development of young karting drivers.

The Mini ROK class uses the Vortex Mini ROK engine, a proven and balanced platform designed specifically for young drivers. The purpose of the class is to give drivers a consistent and reliable foundation from which they can develop throttle control, race rhythm, confidence, racecraft, and the ability to compete in close wheel-to-wheel racing.

Our Focus

The structure of the class is built around fairness. Every driver competes using the same engine platform and within the same controlled technical framework. The objective is not to create an engine development contest, but to place the emphasis where it belongs: on driving ability, preparation, race execution, discipline, and decision-making.

Parents, mechanics, entrants, and teams play an important role in maintaining this environment. A calm, fair, and professional paddock helps the drivers enjoy the experience and develop in the right way.

The intention is simple: to provide safe, competitive, and memorable racing where young drivers can improve, compete fairly, and leave the circuit having gained valuable experience.

02 Procedure

How the Engine Draw Works

Mini ROK Engine Draw and Handling

The Mini ROK class will operate with an official engine draw system designed to support fair competition and clear technical control.

Each competitor will draw an engine on Friday morning by selecting a number from the draw system. The engine allocated to that number, together with its carburettor, will be issued to the competitor for use during Friday practice. The same engine and carburettor will be reissued on Saturday morning. On Sunday morning, a new engine is drawn from the pool, but the competitor's own carburettor — the same one used on Friday and Saturday — is returned to them to use with the new engine.

The engine will be supplied together with its carburettor and related components. The carburettor will be issued in a recommended starting setup, intended to provide competitors with a practical and reliable baseline from which to begin Friday practice.

Carburettor Adjustment

Carburettor adjustment is permitted within the applicable Mini ROK technical regulations. Competitors, teams, and mechanics may make setup adjustments to the carburettor, provided all components remain compliant with the regulations and the engine and carburettor remain within the permitted specification.

Engine Tampering — Zero Tolerance

The engine and carburettor must remain as the official issued unit. The engine may not be opened, modified, interfered with, or tampered with in any way.

Weekend Engine Schedule

Friday Morning
Engine Draw — First Draw
Each competitor draws a number. The engine assigned to that number is issued together with its carburettor. Competitors are responsible for both from the moment they are signed out.
Friday — All Day
Practice Use
The drawn engine and carburettor are used for the whole of Friday practice. All issued components must be handled with care throughout.
Friday Evening
Engine & Carburettor Return
The engine and carburettor must be returned together as one complete unit. The carburettor must remain fitted to the engine when returned. The unit will be sealed in a bag, the seal number recorded, and stored overnight in the engine trailer under the control of the organiser.
Saturday Morning
Same Engine & Carburettor Reissued
Each competitor receives back the same engine and carburettor they used on Friday. The sealed unit is checked, the seal verified, and reissued for Saturday's racing.
Saturday — All Day
Racing Use
The same engine and carburettor from Friday are used for the full day of Saturday racing.
Saturday Evening
Engine Returns to Pool — Carburettor Sealed Separately
At the end of Saturday, the engine is returned to the general pool and will be redistributed. The carburettor is removed, sealed, and stored separately — it is tagged to that competitor and will be returned to them on Sunday morning.
Sunday Morning
New Engine Draw — Same Carburettor Returned
Each competitor draws a new engine from the pool for Sunday. At the same time, they receive back their own sealed carburettor from Friday and Saturday. The new engine and the competitor's carburettor are used together for Sunday's racing.
Sunday — All Day
Racing Use
The new drawn engine paired with the competitor's own carburettor is used for the full day of Sunday racing.
Summary — What Stays With You

Your carburettor follows you all weekend — Friday, Saturday, and Sunday. The engine changes after Saturday: you get a freshly drawn engine on Sunday. This ensures the carburettor setup you develop across the weekend remains consistent while the engine platform stays controlled and fair.

By following this process, all competitors assist in maintaining a fair and controlled racing environment.

03 Collection Procedure

Engine Draw Notes

Official Engine Collection Procedure

Competitors must attend engine collection at the official time published in the event programme or communicated by the organiser.

The engine draw will take place on Friday morning. Each competitor, entrant, or authorised mechanic will draw a number, and the engine allocated to that number will be issued for use.

Competitors must arrive at engine collection with the necessary equipment, tools, and personnel required to fit the engine correctly and safely.

No engine will be considered officially issued until the relevant documentation has been completed and the engine has been signed for by the entrant or an authorised representative.

Entrant or Authorised Mechanic Signature

Important — Signature Requirements

An entrant's signature is required at the time of the engine draw. If a mechanic signs for and receives the engine on behalf of the entrant, the technical team will regard that mechanic as acting with the entrant's full authority.

  • The mechanic must know the driver's name, race number, and class before attempting to collect the engine.
  • Once signed out, the competitor and entrant are fully responsible for the engine and all issued components.

Items Included With the Engine

When signing for the engine, the following items may be issued as part of the engine package. All items must be returned together at the required time.

05 Integrity Policy

Tampering Policy

Once an engine has been drawn and handed over, the competitor and entrant are responsible for looking after it until it is returned. The engine must be used as supplied.

Zero Tolerance — No Exceptions

No opening, internal modification, unauthorised interference, or tampering is permitted under any circumstances. If an entrant, competitor, mechanic, or team member is found to have interfered with the engine or any controlled component in an unauthorised manner, a non-negotiable fine of R10,000 may apply, and exclusion from the event is highly likely.

Carburettor setup adjustments are permitted only within the applicable technical regulations. This does not allow any unauthorised modification to the engine, carburettor body, controlled components, seals, or any specified no-go dimensions.

Tampering Fine
R10 000
Non-negotiable. Applied to any entrant, competitor, or team member found to have interfered with the engine or controlled components in an unauthorised manner. Exclusion from the event is also highly likely. This policy exists to protect every competitor equally.

Looking After the Engine

The engine is official controlled equipment and must be returned in the same condition in which it was issued, subject only to normal use during the event.

  • If something does not feel correct, report it to the technical team immediately.
  • Do not attempt to repair, open, modify, or interfere with the engine yourself.
  • The purpose of the engine system is to keep racing fair and focused on driver performance.
06 Technical Issues

Reporting Engine Issues

What to Do if You Suspect a Problem

If a competitor believes there is an issue with the engine during the event, the kart must be brought to the technical team in parc fermé as soon as possible. Where possible, the engine must remain fitted to the kart and must be presented exactly as it came off the track.

The technical team will inspect the engine and assess whether there is a fault, abnormal condition, or compliance concern. In some cases, competitors may be asked to provide data from their logger to assist with the assessment.

Why Early Reporting Matters

Early reporting helps prevent small issues from becoming larger problems. Competitors should not wait until the end of the day to report an issue that was known earlier. If something does not feel right, it must be reported promptly.

Step-by-Step Engine Issue Reporting Process

  1. 1
    Do not remove the engine
    Leave the engine fitted to the kart exactly as it came off the track.
  2. 2
    Proceed directly to parc fermé
    Go to the parc fermé or technical control area without delay.
  3. 3
    Report to an official
    Speak to a race official or member of the technical team immediately.
  4. 4
    Explain the issue clearly
    Describe what happened, including when the issue occurred and what the driver or mechanic noticed.
  5. 5
    Allow technical inspection
    Allow the technical team to inspect the engine. Provide logger data if requested.
  6. 6
    Wait for official feedback
    Do not refit, swap, repair, adjust beyond permitted carburettor setup, or interfere with the engine unless specifically instructed by an official.
07 Changes

Note on Engine Swaps

During National Championship events, competitors may be required to swap engines from the start of official practice. The number of engine swaps or engine changes for an event is not limited to two and may vary at the sole discretion of the organiser and/or promoter.

Competitors may also request one engine change during the event at their own discretion.

Competitor-Initiated Change
R3 000
One permitted per event
Refund if Engine Faulty
Full
Confirmed by technical team inspection
  • Only one competitor-initiated engine change is permitted per event.
  • A maximum of three spare engines per category may be reserved on standby — first-come, first-served.
  • Once all standby engines have been allocated, no further changes will be allowed unless approved by the organiser under exceptional circumstances.
  • All engine swap decisions remain subject to the control of the organiser, promoter, and technical team.
